Section 5

The Building Official

The Building Official is the engineering office of the Municipality. It receives the applications after their review is started, assesses the fees, approves or declines the application, and prints the permit. This section shows the Dashboard and the For Print pages of the Building Official.

Account. The role of the account is shown at the top left of every page, next to the logo. For this role it reads BUILDING OFFICIAL.

5.1 The Dashboard

The Building Official signs in with the sign in page of Section 2. The first page is the Engineering Dashboard, titled Building Permit. It has five cards. Each card has a switch, a search box and, for the first three, a number in a blue circle that counts the applications in it. Click the switch of a card to show or hide its list. Type a number, a type or a name in the search box to find an application. Click Scan QR at the top right to open an application by scanning its QR code.

Figure 5.1. The Engineering Dashboard. The role is boxed in red.

Building Permit

This card lists the applications that wait for the Building Official. The columns are Date, Owner, Project Title, Location and Actions. Click the arrows beside Date, Owner and Location to sort the list. The column Actions has five icons:

  • Arrow icon (Remarks): shows the remarks that the offices wrote about the application.
  • Paperclip icon (Attachments): opens the requirements that the applicant uploaded.
  • Check box icon (Assessments): opens the assessment of the fees of the application.
  • Double check icon (Approve Permit): approves the application.
  • Box with an X icon (Disapproved Permit): declines the application.
Figure 5.2. The Building Permit card. The five icons of Actions are boxed.

Approved Permits

This card lists the applications that the Building Official approved. The columns are Building Permit No., Date, Owner, Project Title, Location and Actions. A permit that has no number yet shows an empty Building Permit No. The column Actions has the arrow icon for the remarks and the paperclip icon for the attachments.

Figure 5.3. The Approved Permits card.

Declined Permits

This card lists the applications that the Building Official declined. The columns are Date, Owner, Location and Actions. The column Actions has the arrow icon for the remarks, where the reason of the decline is, and the paperclip icon for the attachments.

Figure 5.4. The Declined Permits card.

Occupancy Permit and Electrical Permit

The card Occupancy Permit lists the occupancy permit requests. Its columns are Building Permit No., Date, Application No., Project Title, Completion Date, Status and Action. The card Electrical Permit lists the electrical permit requests. Its columns are Date, Application No., Owner, Completion Date, Start Of Installation, Request Status and Action. A list that has nothing to show says No record found.

Figure 5.5. The cards Occupancy Permit and Electrical Permit.

5.2 The sidebar: For Print

The sidebar has the Dashboard and, under Engineering Submenu, the item For Print. Click For Print to show three pages: Building Permits, Occupancy Permits and Electrical Permits. These are the permits that are ready to be printed.

Building Permits

The page Building Permit For Print lists the building permits that are ready for printing. Type a number or a name in the search box to find one. The columns are Date, Owner, No., Type, Status, OR Number, OR Date, FSEC No and Action. The OR Number and the OR Date are the official receipt of the payment of the fees. The FSEC No is the number of the Fire Safety Evaluation Clearance of the permit. The tag in Status shows APPROVED FOR PAYMENT or NOT-SET. The column Action has two buttons:

  • FSEC: opens a window to type the FSEC number and its date of issue.
  • Print: prints the permit.
Figure 5.6. The page Building Permit For Print. The buttons FSEC and Print are boxed.

Occupancy Permits

The page Occupancy Permit For Print lists the occupancy permits that are ready for printing. Type a number or a name in the search box to find one. The columns are Date, Owner, Application No., Occupancy Id, Status, OR Number, OR Date, FSEC No and Action. The bottom of the table shows the rows per page and the page count.

Figure 5.7. The page Occupancy Permit For Print.

Electrical Permits

The page Completed Electrical Permit List lists the electrical permits that are completed. The columns are Date, Application No., Owner, Completion Date, Start Of Installation, Request Status and Action.

Figure 5.8. The page Completed Electrical Permit List.

5.3 Reviewing an application

The Building Official reads the application and reviews each requirement that the applicant uploaded. The Building Official also decides on every requirement: it approves it or declines it.

The remarks of the application

Click the arrow icon of an application in the Building Permit card to open Building Permit Remarks. It shows the remarks that the offices wrote about the application, with the Date, the office in Process From, the Remarks and the Status. When there is none, it says No remarks found. Click the X to close it.

Figure 5.9. The Building Permit Remarks window.

The requirements of the application

Click the paperclip icon of an application to open the page Application Requirements. It has the six groups of requirements at the left: Forms, Plans, Technical Documents, PRC IDs & PTRs, Personal Documents and Certifications. Click a group to show its requirements at the right. Each requirement has a More button. At the top right are three buttons, which are boxed in the figure:

  • Assessments: opens the assessment of the fees of the application.
  • View Reviews: shows the reviews of the offices. It is dimmed until a review exists.
  • Proceed For Printing: sends the approved application to printing. It is dimmed until the fees of the application are assessed.
Figure 5.10. The paperclip icon of the application is boxed.
Figure 5.11. The Application Requirements page. The three buttons at the top right are boxed.

Reading the files

Click More beside a requirement to open its menu. It has three options: Approve, Decline and View Files. Click View Files to open the files that the applicant uploaded, then click the name of a file to open it. When the applicant uploaded no file, the option reads No Files and is dimmed.

Figure 5.12. The menu of a requirement without files. No Files is dimmed.
Figure 5.13. The files of a requirement.

Approving or declining a requirement

Important. The Building Official gives a decision for each requirement. A remark is required for both decisions, and the button Confirm stays dimmed until you type one.
  1. Click More beside the requirement, then click Approve or Decline.
  2. In the window Approve Requirement (or Decline Requirement), type your note in Remarks. For a decline, write what the applicant must correct.
  3. Click Confirm. Click Cancel to leave without a decision.
Figure 5.14. The menu of More. Approve is boxed.

After you confirm, the decision is shown beside the requirement, for example Status: Approved. The option you used is then dimmed in the menu, so the same decision cannot be given twice.

Figure 5.15. The window Approve Requirement. Remarks and Confirm are boxed.
Figure 5.16. The decision is shown beside the requirement.

To decline a requirement, click Decline in the same menu. The window Decline Requirement works the same way: type the reason in Remarks, then click Confirm. The requirement then shows Status: Declined.

Figure 5.17. The window Decline Requirement. Remarks and Confirm are boxed.
Figure 5.18. The requirement shows Status: Declined.

5.4 Application assessment

The assessment is the list of fees that the applicant must pay for the permit. The page is titled Order of Payment. The Building Official prepares it after reviewing the requirements.

Opening the assessment

Click the check box icon, the third icon, in the Actions of the application in the Building Permit card. The page Order of Payment opens. You can also open it from the requirements: click the paperclip icon, the second icon, to open Application Requirements, and click Assessments at the top right of that page.

Figure 5.19. The check box icon, the third icon in Actions, is boxed.
Figure 5.20. Click Assessments on the page Application Requirements.

The Order of Payment page

The page has these parts:

  • Print Assessment at the top right. It is dimmed until at least one fee is assessed.
  • Pending Assessment Fees at the left. These are the fees that the system suggests. See Section 5.4.4.
  • The table of assessed fees under it, with the fees that are already part of the assessment. See Section 5.4.8.
  • Application Summary at the right. Click its arrow to show or hide the details of the application that the fees are computed from. You complete it first, see Section 5.4.3.
  • Create Assessment Form under it, where you add a fee. See Section 5.4.6.
Figure 5.21. The Order of Payment page with suggested fees.

Completing the application summary

Before you add any fee, complete the Application Summary. The fees are computed from it. Click the arrow of Application Summary to open it. The first fields show the application and cannot be changed here: the Owner, the Project Date, the Location and the Character of Occupancy. You complete the rest:

  • Group: click the box and choose the group of the building, from A - Group A to J - Group J. These are the groups of use of the building.
  • Division: choose the division of the group. It is dimmed until you choose a group, and it lists only the divisions of that group, for example A-1 - Division A-1 and A-2 - Division A-2 for Group A.
  • Building Height (m): type the height of the building in meters.
  • Floor Area (sq.m): shows the floor area of the application. Change it if the assessed area is different.
  • Storage Silo Height (m): type the height only when the project has a storage silo.
Figure 5.22. The Application Summary. Group, Division and Building Height are boxed.
Figure 5.23. The choices of Group.
Figure 5.24. The choices of Division for Group A.
Important. Group, division, floor area and building height are required. If one is empty when you click Save Summary, the message Group, division, floor area, and building height are required. appears and nothing is saved.
Figure 5.25. The message when the building height is empty.

When all the required fields are filled in, click Save Summary. The message Application summary saved. appears.

Figure 5.26. The summary is saved. Save Summary is boxed.

Pending Assessment Fees

When the applicant uploaded plans, the system reads them and suggests the fees that apply. The suggestions are not part of the assessment yet. The red number beside Pending Assessment Fees counts the suggestions that wait for your decision. Click the arrow of the panel to show or hide them. Each suggestion shows:

  • The fee item, for example 8.a, Balconies, terraces, lanais and similar building parts open on two or more sides.
  • Basis, Base and Rate, and the Proposed amount that they give.
  • The Source: the file of the applicant, the page or pages it was found on, and the Confidence of the reading.
  • The link View Attachment or Download Attachment, to check the file yourself.
  • A note in red that explains what the system found in the plan and what you must verify.

Check each suggestion against the plan, then decide:

  • Accept: adds the fee to the assessment. The message Pending assessment fee accepted. appears, and the number of pending fees goes down by one.
  • Remove: discards the suggestion. The message Pending assessment fee removed. appears.
Figure 5.27. A suggested fee. Accept and Remove are boxed.

Reading a proposed fee

Read each proposed fee from top to bottom. The figure shows one fee, and its parts are boxed:

  1. The title. It starts with the number of the fee in the fee table, for example 4.a, followed by the description of the fee.
  2. Basis. This is the quantity that the system read from the plan and that the amount is computed from. It can be a number of items, an area, a cost or a load, depending on the fee. Here the basis is 1.00: one electrical panel.
  3. Base and Rate. Both come from the fee table of the Municipality. The base is a fixed amount for the fee. The rate is the amount for each unit of the basis. Here the base is 200.00 and the rate is 0.00, so the fee is a fixed 200.00.
  4. Proposed amount. This is the amount that the fee would add to the assessment, computed from the base, the rate and the basis. Here it is 200.00.
  5. Source. This is the file that the system read, the page or pages where it found the item, and the Confidence of the reading. A confidence of 100% means that the system is sure of what it read. A lower value means that you must check the plan more carefully.
  6. Download Attachment or View Attachment. Click it to open the source file. A PDF is downloaded, and an image is opened on the page.
  7. The note in red. This is what the system found: the Item, the evidence in the plan, the unit that was counted, and what you must verify. Here it says that one electrical panel is visible in the electrical layout.
Figure 5.28. A proposed fee. Its parts are boxed, from the title to the note in red.

Then decide. Open the source file and compare the quantity with the plan.

  • If the item, the quantity and the fee are right, click Accept. The system calculates the amount again with the current rates of the fee table when it adds the fee, so check the amount in the table of assessed fees.
  • If the quantity is wrong, or the fee does not apply, click Remove. If the amount is wrong, add the fee yourself with the Create Assessment Form, see Section 5.4.6.
  • The same item can appear more than once when it was found in more than one file. Accept it once and remove the other.

Creating an assessment

To add a fee yourself, use the Create Assessment Form.

  1. Make sure the Application Summary is saved.
  2. Click Fee Category and choose a category. A category that is already in the assessment is dimmed, so the same fee is not added twice.
  3. The form shows the fields of the category. Fill them in. The amount is computed from the rate tables of the system.
  4. Check the Assessment Amount. It is filled in with the computed amount, and you can change it.
  5. Click Save to add the fee. A message confirms that the assessment is saved, and the fee appears in the table of assessed fees with the new TOTAL. Click Cancel to leave without adding it.

When there are no suggestions left, the panel Pending Assessment Fees says No pending assessment fees. The note under the form says that saving keeps the selected group, division, floor area and building height in step with the application summary.

Figure 5.29. The Create Assessment Form. Click Fee Category to choose a category.
Figure 5.30. The choices of Fee Category. The categories that are already assessed are dimmed.

The categories are:

  • Building Fees: computed from the group, the division and the floor area of the application.
  • Accessory Structure Fees: computed from the accessory structure fee table and the values of the application summary. Check Apply this fee on each row that applies.
  • Accessory Fees: enter the quantity or the size that each row needs, for example for signs.
  • Electrical Fees: see Section 5.4.7.
  • Mechanical Fees: enter the quantity of each item. The rate is taken from the table.
  • Plumbing Fees: enter the number of fixtures installed. Water meter fees use the size of the meter.
  • Electronics Fees: enter the quantity of each item, which is multiplied by its rate.
  • Certificate of Occupancy Fees and Annual Inspection Fees: each has its own calculator.
  • Surcharge and penalties, Filing Fees and Processing Fees: the other fees of the permit.

In the categories with many rows, type in Search displayed fees to filter the rows by section or description.

Example: Electrical Fees

The form Electrical Assessment has these fields. Each amount field shows its computed amount below it:

  • Total Connected Load and UPS/Generator Capacity: type the value. The base fee is taken from the table.
  • Pole/Attachment Location Plan Permit: type the number of poles in Power Supply Pole Location and the number of attachments in Guying Attachment. The rate is 30.00 for each.
  • Miscellaneous Fees: check the fee that applies, grouped by use: Residential, Commercial_Industrial or Institutional. Each group has an Electrical Meter fee and a Wiring Meter fee.
Figure 5.31. The form Electrical Assessment. The loads, the pole and the attachment are filled in.
Figure 5.32. The attachment and the miscellaneous fees. All the meter fees are checked.

Below the fees, Computed Assessment Basis and Computed Amount Due show the total of what you entered. The box Assessment Amount starts with that amount, and you can type another amount, for example to round it. The note under it reads: Editable amount. The database calculation is used as the default.

In the example, the fees add up to a computed amount of 468.00, so the box starts with 468. To charge a different amount, type it in the box, for example 490.

Figure 5.33. The Assessment Amount starts with the computed amount, 468.
Figure 5.34. Another amount, 490, typed in the Assessment Amount. Assessment Amount and Save are boxed.

After you click Save, the form is cleared, the message that the assessment is saved appears, and the fee is added to the table of assessed fees. The TOTAL is updated. In the example, the amount 468.00 was saved, so the table shows Electrical Fees with 468.00 and a TOTAL of 6,292.00.

Figure 5.35. The Electrical Fees are added to the assessed fees. The new row is boxed.

The assessed fees

The table of assessed fees lists what is already in the assessment. Its columns are Assessed Fees, Account Code, Amount Due, Assessed By and Action. The last row, TOTAL, is the sum that the applicant must pay. In Action, click the edit icon to change a fee, or the trash icon to remove it. When at least one fee is assessed, Print Assessment is enabled. Click it to print the order of payment.

Figure 5.36. The assessed fees. The edit icon, the trash icon and Print Assessment are boxed.

Editing and removing a fee

To edit a fee, click the edit icon of the fee in the table of assessed fees. The form below changes to the fee: it shows the computed amount and the Assessment Amount. Change the amount, then click Update. Click Cancel to leave without a change. The message assessment updated. appears, and the amount of the fee and the TOTAL are updated.

Figure 5.37. The edit icon of a fee.
Figure 5.38. The form of an edit. The Assessment Amount is changed to 50. Update is boxed.
Figure 5.39. The fee and the TOTAL are updated.

To remove a fee, click the trash icon of the fee. The window Delete assessment asks Are you sure you want to delete this assessment? Click Delete to remove the fee, or Cancel to keep it. The message Assessment removed. appears, the fee leaves the table and the TOTAL is updated.

Figure 5.40. The window Delete assessment.
Figure 5.41. The fee is removed and the TOTAL is updated.

Printing the order of payment

Click Print Assessment at the top right of the Order of Payment. The page Print Assessment shows the order of payment as it will be printed. It has these parts:

  • The heading of the Office of the Building Official, with the logos and a QR code.
  • The owner, the proposed project and the location, and, at the right, the division, the group and the date.
  • The fee description and the assessed amount of each fee, and the TOTAL.
  • Official Receipt No. and Date Paid. They show N/A until the fees are paid.
  • The breakdown of the collection derived from the building fees and other charges: the eighty percent (80%) share of the Local Government Unit, the fifteen percent (15%) share of the Office of the Building Official, and the five percent (5%) share of the NCBOD.
  • The floor area, the additional floor area and the total floor area.
  • The signature lines: Reviewed and recommended for payment by the Chief, Processing and Evaluation Division, and For payment by the Building Official.

Click Print Assessment on this page to open the print window of the browser. Choose a printer, or save the page as a PDF. Click Close to go back to the Order of Payment.

Figure 5.42. The page Print Assessment. The button Print Assessment is boxed.
Figure 5.43. The print window of the browser.

5.5 Approving or declining the permit

When the requirements are reviewed and the fees are assessed, the Building Official decides on the permit: it approves it or declines it.

Approving the permit

There are two ways to start the approval:

  • In the Building Permit card, click the double check icon of the application, Approve Permit. Its name appears when you point to it.
  • On the page Application Requirements, click Proceed For Printing. It is dimmed until the fees are assessed, and it is enabled after.
Figure 5.44. The icon Approve Permit, the double check icon.
Figure 5.45. Proceed For Printing is enabled after the fees are assessed.

The window Approved Building Permit Request opens. Fill in two boxes, then click Approved. Click Cancel to leave without approving.

  • Building Permit No.: type the number of the permit.
  • Remarks: type your note.
Figure 5.46. The window Approved Building Permit Request. Building Permit No. and Approved are boxed.

The message Building permit successfully approved. appears. The application leaves the Building Permit card and is listed in Approved Permits with its Building Permit No. In its remarks, the arrow icon shows your note under Process From Approver.

Figure 5.47. The message after the permit is approved.
Figure 5.48. The application is in Approved Permits with its Building Permit No.
Figure 5.49. The remarks of the approved application.

The applicant sees the status of the application change to APPROVED. See Section 3.5.

Declining the permit

To decline, click the box with an X icon of the application in the Building Permit card, Disapproved Permit. The window Declined Building Permit Request opens. Type the reason in Remarks, then click Declined. Click Cancel to leave without declining. The application is then listed in Declined Permits, where its remarks show the reason.

Figure 5.50. The window Declined Building Permit Request. Remarks and Declined are boxed.
